Nordic vs US split: Nordic players (Yazen, Kry, Doktor24) send people to a human first (nurse chat or video booking) with a short quiz before it. Velora works the same way. US players run a long asynchronous intake and go straight to payment, with no live contact. Velora's "free meeting" step is closer to Yazen's free nurse chat. The difference: Yazen asks for an account first, while Velora goes straight to a booked time, which is the stronger hook.

Patterns worth stealing

  1. Promise the effort up front. Numan puts "2–3 minutes" right next to the CTA, which removes the "how long is this?" fear before the first tap. Put "About 2 min · free · no obligation" on screen 1.
  2. Show BMI as a calm number, not a verdict. Yazen shows the BMI and explains it gently, including a guard against an unhealthy target ("20–25 is considered healthiest"). Show the computed BMI with one neutral line about what it means for treatment.
  3. Remove the price fear at the commit point. Numan's "No charge unless treatment is prescribed" and Hims' "$0 consult" do this. For Velora, repeat "The meeting is free and doesn't commit you to anything" on the result screen and the booking screen. Also show what treatment costs (Yazen and Embla print it, medicine excluded). Hidden price is the main complaint in Hims reviews.
  4. Name the humans and their credentials. Numan shows named doctors with GMC numbers, Voy prints its GPhC number, Yazen says it's IVO-registered. Velora equivalent: a photo and name of the nurse or doctor you'll meet, "Legitimerad sjuksköterska", and IVO/vårdgivare registration in the footer of the result and booking screens.
  5. Ask for side-effect tolerance and worries, then answer them. Noom asks whether mild nausea would make you stop, and has a concerns multi-select (cost, judgment, needles). It makes people feel understood rather than interrogated, and the answers can tailor the result screen ("You mentioned needles: there's also a tablet option").
  6. Turn the result into a journey. Calibrate shows a day-by-day timeline (days 1–5 labs, days 7–14 consult) and Found shows week by week. Put a 3–4 step "what happens next" on the result screen: meeting → blood test → doctor → start. This matches the notes.md idea of better visuals on the result screen.
  7. Branch, don't lengthen. Hims asks follow-ups only when they're relevant (e.g. if you're on other meds). Ask "Do you have any of these conditions?" only when BMI is 27–29.9. That keeps the happy path short for BMI ≥30.
  8. State exclusions openly. Doktor24 (BMI >45, diabetes) and Calibrate (pregnancy, eating disorder) list them on the page. It builds trust and screens out the wrong people before the meeting.

Patterns to avoid

  1. Email gate before the result (Noom). It extracts a lead at the moment of highest curiosity, which feels like a trick in a health context. Show the result first and collect contact details at booking, where they're actually needed.
  2. Card or ID before any human contact (Hims), and 55-screen intakes (Noom). Both are fine for async-Rx models but wrong for Velora's goal, which is booking a free meeting. Do the deep medical history in the meeting, not in the quiz.
  3. Discount-led urgency and before/after weight hype (Juniper's Black Friday sale, which ED advocates called out; Yazen's 50% code). It undercuts the "safe and personal" tone, and medicine promotion is regulated in Sweden. Keep the tone clinical-warm.

Eligibility rule (verified)

Confirmed, with nuance. FASS, Wegovy 2.4 mg, section 4.1 (the Swedish SmPC, mirrors EMA): adults with an initial BMI ≥30 kg/m², or **≥27 to <30 kg/m² with at least one weight-related comorbidity** such as dysglycemia (prediabetes or type 2 diabetes), hypertension, dyslipidemia, obstructive sleep apnea or cardiovascular disease. Adolescents from 12 years with obesity and body weight >60 kg are also covered, but that's out of scope (Velora is adults). Stop and reassess if weight loss is under 5% after 12 weeks at the maintenance dose.

  • Source: FASS Wegovy fass-text (opened).
  • Mounjaro (tirzepatide) has the same thresholds and comorbidity list in its EMA SmPC section 4.1. Source: EMA Mounjaro product information (via EMA search result).
  • Providers add their own extra limits: an age band of 18–75 (Embla, Doktor24), BMI >45 or diabetes routed elsewhere (Doktor24), BMI ≥30 only (Kry). These are company policy, not label requirements.
  • Wording for notes.md: "Per the FASS/EMA label (Wegovy, Mounjaro): BMI ≥30, or BMI 27–<30 with ≥1 weight-related condition (prediabetes/T2D, high blood pressure, high blood lipids, sleep apnea, cardiovascular disease). Age limits and other exclusions are provider policy (assumed 18+). The final decision is the clinician's."
  • Quiz implication: the comorbidity question is needed only when BMI is 27–29.9. Under 27 is the non-qualifying path, which is out of scope.

Top 3 takeaways

  1. Velora's model is human-first like the Nordic players, so the quiz should be short (about 8–12 screens) and end in a booked time, not an account or a card. The deep medical history belongs in the free meeting. Say the time cost and "free, no obligation" up front, as Numan does.
  2. Trust comes from specifics, not adjectives. Name and show the clinician, print the registration (IVO, legitimerad), show real price ranges, and list who it isn't for. Every top UK and Nordic player does at least three of these.
  3. The result screen should explain, not judge. Show a calm BMI, a personalised line drawn from the person's own answers (worries, goals), a 3–4 step "what happens next" timeline, and one primary CTA: "Book your free meeting". Don't gate it behind email.
